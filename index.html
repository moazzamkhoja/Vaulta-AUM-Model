① Balances

Consumer earns 3.84% on their whole balance. Chime pays 0% on checking — this is the structural difference and the reason balances here should exceed Chime's.
Share of a month's collections a merchant parks with Vaulta. Vaulta keeps a 30% sleeve on it, the merchant gets 3.36%.

② Payments — one fee, two rails

Charged on every payment whatever the rail. Vaulta is a payment facilitator, not a card issuer, so it earns no interchange. What differs by rail is cost, not the fee.
Direct Solana Pay, ~$0.00025 network fee, no card network: nets 0.50%. The rest runs on the Rain debit card and nets 0.23% after Rain's fee. More QR means a better margin, not a worse one.
Rain platform fee, with the Visa/Mastercard network cost inside it. The merchant's interchange goes to their acquirer and the issuing bank — Vaulta is not in that flow.
Sets transactions per dollar of card spend. At $48: earn $0.24, pay $0.13, net $0.11.
~$5 card amortised over 24 months, on every customer.

Option 1. A fifth segment acquired for tokenized investing, arriving with an opening balance — Wealthfront's motion, at robo CAC and churn. Edit it on the Customers tab.

Rail economics — one transaction at the average ticket

The merchant pays the same fee on both rails. QR settles on Solana for a fraction of a cent; the Rain card carries Rain's per-transaction fee with the card-network cost inside it. Interchange the merchant pays on a card transaction goes to their acquirer and the issuing bank, not to Vaulta.

The three business models

Neither comparable earns most of its money from the thing it is named for. Chime is 69% interchange because it is the card issuer through a bank partner; Wealthfront is 74% deposit spread. Vaulta is neither — its equivalent primary line is NIM on the T-bill sleeve. The 0.25% advisory fee is a quarter of Wealthfront's revenue even though its clients hold $26,071 of invested assets.
